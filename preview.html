05

New Zealand wouldn't be doing this alone.

Several democracies have tried, are trying, or have already enacted laws holding politicians accountable for misinformation. Some are good models. Some are cautionary tales. An honest assessment of each.

Wales

Enacted, April 2026

Senedd Cymru (Member Accountability and Elections) Act 2026

Passed the Senedd 50 votes to 1 on 17 March 2026, Royal Assent 27 April. It introduces recall, reforms the standards regime, and requires Welsh Ministers to create a criminal offence of false or misleading statements at Senedd elections.

Narrower than this bill: scoped to election periods, and the offence itself is still to be made by regulation. But the "no Western democracy has done this" objection no longer holds.

United Kingdom

Amendment stage

"Compassion in Politics" amendment

A cross-party amendment would create a standalone offence for MPs who intentionally or recklessly mislead the public. No bill formally introduced. The existing convention is that ministers who mislead Parliament resign, with no legal sanction.

Singapore

Cautionary

POFMA 2019

Ministers can order corrections directly. Criticised internationally as a tool used disproportionately against opposition and dissenters. A model of what to avoid: this bill puts the power with an independent officer, not a minister.

04

The penalty ladder.

Graduated, so a first slip is a warning and prison is reserved for people who keep doing it on purpose.

  1. 1

    Warning

    Formal, published. First finding.

  2. 2

    Public censure

    Reported to the House. A correction is required.

  3. 3

    Fine

    $10,000 to $50,000, escalating on repetition.

  4. 4

    Enhanced fine

    $50,000 to $100,000 for a sustained pattern.

  5. 5

    Criminal prosecution

    Referred to the Solicitor-General. On conviction: up to $200,000, up to 2 years, possible disqualification from office.

It is illegal to lie to your employer, your bank, your insurer, and the court. Not to the people who elect you.
